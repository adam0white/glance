## Her report


## 1. First 60 seconds

I understood it straight away, which surprised me, because I have never played one of these. A drawing of a dam with a crest line, a spillway lip, a staff gauge and a '79 flood mark on the wall; a hut; two dials; a log with times down the side. That is a control room. "Every figure on your board is what he saw" is the truest sentence in the thing, and anyone who has sat a night desk knows it. What confused me: the three little arrows at the top right. I did not know they were speed until I pressed one and an hour went by in twenty seconds. Nothing on the page tells you the game runs by itself while you sit there, and that matters more than anything else on the screen. I also could not tell you where 4.0 m sits against the lip or the crest, because the staff on the drawing has ticks and no numbers.

## 2. People or dice?

People, mostly, and the right sort. Dai is every night man I ever had on the radio. "(a long yawn) three-eight, bang on." "Kettle's on. Shout if you want me." "Ground's full. Whatever falls now comes straight down." That last one is exactly what a keeper says when the catchment is saturated, and it came at 01:20 on Tuesday without my asking, which is when they say it. "About four-five. Torch is going." I have heard that. The pencilled note in the margin, "Dai reads low after three. —M.", is the best thing in the game: a previous desk man leaving you the one fact you need.

What felt wrong: Monday 04:33, I pressed him for three turns while the level was at the lip and he said "I can't. Arms are gone. Give me twenty." He had not touched the wheel all night. Arms gone from what? Reading a staff gauge does not take your arms. And "Give me twenty" on a rising reservoir is the line you remember at the inquiry. At dawn he told me "I said no once. You took it. Good." I gave him his twenty and then asked again; that is managing him, not obeying him, and he should know the difference.

Nerys at the Bridge Inn is right. "Mm? What time is it… Hang on, I'll look." then "It's at the second step. Bit brown." and later "Third step, love. It's moving." River people count steps and they tell you the colour. Callum, the new lad: "Ready for anything" at 22:03 and "Sorry, sir. The baby's teething. I'm fine." at 00:30 is a lad I would send to the weir and not to the wheel.

One thing that is dice, not people: of thirteen readings over three nights, nine were wrong, and every one of the nine was low, by 0.3 to 0.6 m, never high. A tired man reads low once or twice and high once. Half a metre low every time is not tiredness, it is a bent staff or a man who has stopped looking. Either make the error smaller and sometimes high, or let me find out the staff is wrong.

## 3. The sharpest choice I made, and what came of it

Tuesday, 22:13, before the rain came on. I had him put the gate on two turns while the reservoir was still at 4.2 and falling, then three at midnight. You draw down ahead of the storm, not during it; that is the whole job. The level went 4.2 to 3.8 to 3.7 by half past midnight and I had room. At 04:53 I asked for five turns, and I rang Nerys straight after; I should have rung first. She said "third step, it's moving" at 05:01 and I judged Mill Lane could stand it. The dam went over its lip and the spillway ran, the chart at dawn showed the water peaking just over the lip and well under the crest, and Mill Lane slept dry. That is a night I would sign. The game let me do the right thing and showed me, in a chart I would frame, that it was the right thing.

The same order on Wednesday did nothing, and that is in the bugs.

## 4. Bugs and layout problems

- Wednesday 00:56: I asked for three turns from the gate dial. Dai said "Going now", the card said "walking to the wheel". At 00:59 I tapped his card and asked him to get his head down; he said "After I open the gate, then I will." The dawn chart shows the gate flat on one turn all night and the dam going over the crest at about 04:00. He never turned it, and nothing on the board told me so. I sat watching the clock and the dam went over.
- The clock moves when the page is shut. Tuesday I closed it at about 03:58, reopened twenty-four seconds later and it read 04:52. A save should hold still. If the desk runs while I am away, say so on the title page.
- The opposite also happens: Monday I closed at 03:37 and reopened at 03:32; Tuesday closed 23:49, reopened 23:46. It loses the last few minutes. Pick one.
- Monday 04:23, the drawing: Dai was drawn asleep ("z") at the staff gauge on the dam face, below the water line, while the hut sat empty and the card said "not answering". He had walked back "to the hut" at 03:55.
- Monday 03:34, gate dial while he slept: all six buttons said "can't", but above them his quote still read "I can, but the next round will be slow." A sleeping man does not say that.
- Sometimes a reading is dated half an hour before he says it: Monday the dial said "Dai 22:39" and he spoke at 23:09; "Dai 04:44", spoke at 05:14. Other times (01:39, 03:52) the two matched. If that is the walk back from the gauge, the hut is next to the dam, and it should be every time or never.
- Title page: tapping the empty top-right corner, where the speed buttons sit on the desk page, took me off to the framework's main site. A grandson would not come back.
- Tuesday 23:44: tapping the words "Bridge Inn" on the keeper card did nothing; tapping the card itself worked. Same for Callum.
- Wednesday dawn chart: the houses of Mill Lane and the square are filled blue although the week screen says Mill Lane dry. Blue means wet to anyone who has drawn a flood map.
- Wednesday dawn chart stops at 04:00 but the axis runs to 06. Fine if that is when it went over, but say so.
- After midnight the header still says Monday. Shift date, I know, but a stranger will think the clock is broken.
- The weir sheet had "Dai by the stove / Callum by the stove" written above "Send Dai to the weir". I did not know the names were buttons; nothing said so. I went through Callum's own card instead.

## 5. My demands, ranked

1. Make the save a save. Nothing moves while the page is shut, or tell me plainly that it does.
2. Make a gate order visible on the board while it is being carried out, and tell me if it fails. Wednesday's lost order cost the dam.
3. Numbers on the staff gauge in the drawing, or at least a figure for the lip and the crest. I cannot judge "five-one" against a dashed line.
4. Let me catch a wrong figure. "You caught none of them" three dawns running, and I was never shown how I could have. A second reading, the camera in the lightning, Nerys's steps against Dai's metres: give me a way to say "that's wrong" and have it count.
5. A rate. Any desk has the previous reading beside the current one; I want to see "+0.3 in 1h15" without a pencil.
6. Pre-release should be praised. Opening to two before the storm on Tuesday saved it; let Dai say so at dawn instead of "I didn't keep my watch".
7. A fourth and fifth night. Three is not a week.

## 6. Verdict

It understands what a night desk is, and Dai is a real keeper; if the save stopped moving behind my back and the gate did what he said it would, I would play the whole week twice.

7 out of 10.
