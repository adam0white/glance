# Playtest: Priya, 2026-10-19

Source-unread playtest of the live build (commit 4e2f35a, HF 2.7.0) on a 360x780 mobile context (headless Chromium, Playwright). Persona: Priya, 29, a data analyst who min-maxes strategy games. Model: Sonnet (the build used Opus). Her words are recorded verbatim below.

## What changed because of it

Applied in 8e870e7 and the commit after it:
- Every gate and keeper button shows its cost in minutes ("· ~25 min").
- The level dial carries lip and crest ticks.
- The gate-check loop is gone: a keeper only offers to check the gate when a gate order is pending.
- Weir and decimal figures are spoken correctly ("one point nine eight").
- Dawn states the rule for a caught reading.
- Board stamps show when he looked; the slip between looking and telling is now clamped to 30 minutes.
- HF 2.8.0 per-offer forecast thresholds make "the next round will be slow" depend on the actual exertion of each order.
Deferred (docs/design.md): a per-keeper bias readout, a weather forecast and a what-if replay.

## Her report

## 1. First 60 seconds

I understood the premise quickly. I'm the night desk, and everything on my board is "what Dai saw". The gauges grey out as their readings age, and each carries a stamp like "Dai 22:30". That told me the game is about information, not about the dam. I liked it.

Four things confused me:
- **Dai's claim of accuracy.** He said "bang on" about a figure that turned out to be off by 0.4 m. His words give no hint of how reliable the number is.
- **How slow he is.** A read takes 20 to 30 minutes of game time, and a gate change takes about 40. Nothing told me that up front.
- **The panel footer.** "I can, but the next round will be slow." stays on every panel at the start of a night. I couldn't tell whether it was a warning or boilerplate.
- **My first save.** I opened the game, closed it after 1.5 seconds, and my first run did not come back as "Back to the desk". After a longer session it resumed fine. Early saves may not have been written yet.

## 2. People or dice?

Mostly people, and that is the best thing here. These lines worked:
- Dai, after I set the gate: "Not that — I'll check the gate." He refused a level read because he was worried about the gate.
- Dai, on Tuesday: "Ground's full. Whatever falls now comes straight down." This was unprompted, and it was the best hint in the game. I acted on it too softly.
- Callum: "Weir board, nought point five two, sir. I think it's nought point five two." The nervous double-check is a good tell.
- Callum: "Sorry, sir. The baby's teething. I'm fine."
- Dai, asleep: "You sent me to sleep on watch. Thirty years nobody did that."
- Dai, hedging: "(a long yawn) five-one… I think."
- The pencilled log margin: "Dai reads low after three. —M." It was true in my data. Dai's after-midnight readings were low by 0.3 to 0.6 m, and the debrief confirmed it.

These lines felt like dice or templates:
- **Repeated closing lines.** "Old dam held. She usually does." appeared in two different debriefs. "I didn't keep my watch. Next time I'll be ready when it comes." appeared in every debrief, even on a night when the dam went over.
- **Wrong tone.** Dai said "steady as you like" right after a 0.3 m fall.
- **Number-word bug.** Callum said "nought point 19 eight? It could be higher" and "nought point 13 nine". The board correctly showed 1.98 and 1.39 m, so only the speech is garbled.
- **Hedges that aren't reliable.** Callum said "it could be higher" about 1.98, and the true value was 1.74. If the hedge is meant to tell me which way he is wrong, it doesn't.
- **Gate-check loop.** After any gate change, each keeper answers every other request with "rather check the gate" or "I'll check the gate instead", over and over. Wednesday cost me roughly three hours of game time. That reads as a priority queue, not a person.
- **Sleep loop.** Dai says "Not now. After I sit by the stove." while his status already reads "by the stove". I tried sitting him down first. He still said "After I sit by the stove, then I will."

## 3. The sharpest choice

**Tuesday, 02:37.** Dai had read 4.0 at 00:47 and 4.4 at 02:47. I set the gate to 3 at 01:56 and left Dai asleep after 03:54, when his last read was 5.1. Nerys then said "Fourth step. That's high. Mill Lane gets it at five." I rang her again at 04:36 and she said "It's in the lane!" I held "Warn Mill Lane", but it was too late.

At first light, Mill Lane had flooded. The debrief said "The dam went over its lip and the spillway ran." The chart showed the true level crossing the lip at about 02:15 and peaking around 5.4 at 03:00, while Dai kept reporting 5.0 to 5.1. I misread the scale. I thought the lip was about 6 m. It is about 5.2.

**Wednesday, 23:42.** With a second keeper, Callum, I opened the gate to 3 early, then to 6, and sent Callum to the weir for upstream readings. I drained the dam to 3.2 m true. Mill Lane slept dry. But the bridge sat just under the Mill Lane line all night, so I was one rainfall away from flooding the town with my own gate.

**Thursday.** I did the same early opening and cleared a tree off the screen with Callum. The dam still went over the lip, because my gate-6 order landed at 03:09, an hour late. Gate 6 also pushed the bridge past the Mill Lane line at about 03:30, which is where the Mill Lane flood came from. That tradeoff between dam and town is real, and I think it's good design.

## 4. Bugs and layout problems

- **Gate panel off-screen with two keepers.** With Dai and Callum both present (Wednesday onward), the Gate panel's number buttons were at y=775 to 857 on a 780 px screen. They were unreachable without scrolling. When I scroll, the speed buttons leave the viewport. I tapped at y=800 and nothing happened until I worked out the page was scrolling.
- **Keeper menu overflow.** The Dai and Callum menus (Read the level, Go to the weir, Sit by the stove, Get your head down, Clear the screen) are also taller than the viewport on Thursday.
- **Report time vs read time.** The gauge showed "Dai 23:15" for a level he reported at 23:45. The stamp is when he looked, not when he told me. That is probably deliberate, but it is not obvious.
- **Wednesday 23:15 to 23:20.** Dai said "Gate's on one turn" twice. Callum, at 23:16, said "she won't go further. She's on two turns." Two witnesses disagreed and I couldn't resolve it.
- **Debrief sleep list.** On Tuesday it listed Dai's sleep only as 00:15 to 00:53, not the sleep I ordered at 03:54. The "You sent me to sleep on watch" line fits the one I ordered, so the listing looks incomplete.
- **Debrief wording.** "Mill Lane flooded before anyone knocked" appeared on Thursday even though I had warned at 05:01. The flood started around 03:30, so the wording is accurate.
- **"You caught it" has no rule I can see.** I never cross-checked anything, and it still credited me with catches on Monday.

## 5. My demands, ranked

1. **Show the cost of each action before I commit.** Say how long a read or a gate change will take. I want latency in numbers. "Slow" tells me nothing I can optimise.
2. **Mark where the lip is on the gauge, and say when a reading is past it.** Tuesday's 5.1 was already over, and the game did not tell me.
3. **Fix the gate-check loop.** Let me cancel a pending "check the gate". Or make it one check per gate change, not several.
4. **Make Dai's bias legible, or let me measure it.** The margin note was the one thing that taught me. A per-night readout of how far off each keeper tends to be would let me correct for it. Right now it is invisible until the debrief.
5. **Let me see the weather plan.** The lightning flashes on the chart suggest a schedule, and I'd like a forecast. Is each night different? Monday and Tuesday had no radio dropouts. Wednesday and Thursday varied. I can't tell what is luck and what is my play.
6. **Fix the panel overflow** so the gate buttons are reachable at 360x780.
7. **Add a replay or "what if" view.** The debrief chart is excellent, but it shows only what happened. I'd pay for seeing the same night with the gate at 6 from 22:00.

## 6. Verdict

A moving, honest game about knowing less than you think. I'd keep playing for the information puzzle, but the reaction delays and the unreadable "how wrong could he be" kill the optimisation I came for. 7/10.
